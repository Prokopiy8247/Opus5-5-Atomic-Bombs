### Disputes resolved
- **Yield 44 t (claimed) vs ~15–20 t (implied by Coyle/Lange):** the only number with any provenance is Russia's 44 t; there is no independent measurement. Game uses 44 t but must display it as "заявлено Россией, не подтверждено" and the info card mentions the doubts. Note: 44 t from ~7 t of fill implies ~6× TNT per kg, which is possible only if most energy comes from burning fuel with air (heat of combustion of hydrocarbon/ethylene-oxide fuels is ~6–10× TNT's energy), so it is not physically impossible, but blast-equivalent yields of FAEs are usually lower than their heat release.
- **Mass 7.1 t vs ~8 t:** use 7.1 t (most-cited), UI "≈7 т".
- **Air vs ground burst:** fuel-air cloud lies on/near the ground → burst 10 m, hemispherical ground-hugging fireball. This also reconciles the "air burst" narration with footage that looks like a ground explosion.

### Visual signature (game)
1. **t = −20 s … 0:** grey cylindrical bomb under a single drogue/ribbon parachute, descending nose-down-ish at ~30–50 m/s (parachute-retarded). No aircraft in frame.
2. **0 – 0.15 s (dispersal):** a dull "pop" at ~10–20 m above ground; a pale grey-white aerosol pancake balloons out to Ø ≈ 100 m, ≈ 10–15 m thick (semi-transparent, like fog).
3. **0.15 – 0.25 s (detonation):** the whole pancake flashes yellow-white almost at once (detonation sweeps the cloud in ~50 ms); brief Wilson condensation dome possible in humid air; very bright but much less "blinding" than a nuclear flash (no exposure blow-out; drop exposure ~2 stops).
4. **0.25 – 1.5 s:** a broad, flattened, rolling dome of yellow-orange flame grows to Ø ≈ 120 m (up to 160 m), ≈ 70 m tall; texture = turbulent flame tongues, NOT a smooth sphere. A tan/brown dust ring is kicked up and races outward along the ground (see BLU-82 photo); visible shock "shimmer" ring.
5. **1.5 – 5 s:** flame colour shifts orange → deep orange-red; thick black soot rolls appear inside and on top (fuel-rich burning); the fireball lifts off the ground and rolls into a short-stemmed mushroom / torus (Russian footage shows "fireball with the start of a mushroom").
6. **5 – 8 s:** last flames die inside the rising dark grey-black smoke ball; brown dust skirt Ø ≈ 400–600 m around the base.
7. **8 s – 3 min:** smoke/dust column rises at ~15–20 m/s, top ≈ 2 km by ~3 min, cap Ø ≈ 0.5 km; smoke greys and drifts with wind.
8. **Blast & sound:** destruction ring 300 m (claimed); windows to ≈ 0.8 km (1 psi at 0.78 km); long positive phase ("push" rather than "crack"); at 10 km sound arrives after ≈ 29 s as a deep, long rumble.
Palette: flash #FFF4C8 → flame #FFB030 / #FF7A10 → smoke #2A2522 / #4A4440 → dust #B59A78.

### Disputes resolved
- **"Official" yields:** there are none. Only NNSA's "substantial reduction in yield". The 0.3 kt minimum is a well-established non-official estimate (FAS/NWA, inherited from B61-3/-4 settings). UI must say «оценка, официально засекречено».
- **Burst height 100 m vs 150 m:** both are fictional for this weapon. 150 m reproduces the scaled height of Wasp/Able (≈ 230–240 m·kt^(−1/3)), so their real photos/films are valid look references (detached cap + thin dust column + low precursor dust skirt on desert). At 100 m the dust column would be somewhat stronger; either is defensible — recommend 150 m.
- **Fireball 40–55 m (01 ref) vs 38 m:** 40–55 m is the 1-kt range; for 0.3 kt use 38 m.

### Visual signature (game; desert test-range setting)
1. **t < 0:** slim white/grey bomb with red-orange mid-band falls nose-down under guided-tail control (no parachute), ~250–300 m/s.
2. **0 – 1.2 ms:** blue-white point flash; whole screen white-out (exposure −6 stops).
3. **1.2 ms – 5 ms:** brief brightness dip (thermal minimum): opaque orange-red shell Ø ≈ 35 m, dark centre.
4. **~23 ms (second peak):** brilliant white sphere Ø ≈ 55–65 m and growing; hard shadows on the ground; Wilson condensation dome flicker possible in humid air (≈0.3–0.5 s, radius ≈ 150–250 m·W^(1/3) ≈ 100–170 m).
5. **0.05 – 0.15 s:** fireball reaches Ø ≈ 75 m, colour white → yellow; lumpy (rapatronic) surface; at 150 m height it never touches the ground.
6. **0.15 – 1 s:** fireball yellow-orange → orange-red → dull red, starts rising and flattening into a torus; on the desert a low tan dust skirt (precursor) spreads under it, radius ≈ 300–400 m; shock-wave "shimmer" ring, Mach-stem kink at ≥ 150 m ground range.
7. **1 – 15 s:** glowing core fades; a compact, lumpy **reddish-brown (nitrogen-oxide) cloud ball** rises (see Able colour photo) at ~30–40 m/s; a thin dust column rises from the ground to ~300–500 m but does **not** join the cap (Wasp photo).
8. **15 s – 4 min:** cap rises to ≈ 2 km above the burst, widens to Ø ≈ 0.9 km, turns from red-brown to grey-white; short ragged tendrils hang under it; drifts with wind. Normalised rise (z/H): 0.27 at ~17 s, 0.67 at ~60 s, 0.8 at ~90 s, 1.0 at ~240 s.
9. **Sound:** at 12 km view distance the shock/sound arrives after ≈ 35 s — a sharp crack then rumble.

### Disputes resolved
- **Burst height 580 vs 600 m:** 580 m (1,900 ft) is the design fuze setting and the DS86 value; the 2002 DS02 reassessment (US–Japan, adopted by RERF) moved it to 600 m to match neutron-activation measurements. Use **600 m**.
- **Yield 15 vs 16 kt:** 15 kt is the LANL (Malik 1985) best estimate (±20 %) and the most-cited value; DS02 uses 16 kt. The difference changes fireball radius by ~2.6 % (W^0.4) — invisible. Use **15 kt** in physics, UI text «≈15 кт (оценки 12–18 кт)».
- **Fireball size:** 280 m Ø is Nagasaki's figure; Hiroshima official ">200 m radius at 1 s", Wikipedia 370 m Ø, formula 360 m Ø. Use **R_max 190 m at ~1 s**.
- **Cloud height 3 km@48 s / 9 km@8.5 min / 12–17 km:** "3 km at ~45 s" fits the physics curve; "9 km" fits ~3 min (secondary US record), not 8.5 min — at 8.5 min the cloud was already ≈ 12 km (crew saw it ~10,000 ft above their 31,000 ft within a few minutes). 16–17 km comes from a high-uncertainty photo analysis and/or overshoot; 8 km is an older underestimate. Use **12.5 km stabilised at ~10 min**, with an optional brief overshoot to 13.5 km at ~6–8 min.
- **Number of shocks felt by Enola Gay:** two strong (direct + ground reflection) is supported by Caron and DOE; the "smaller third" comes only from the 509th narrative. Use 2 jolts.
- **Iconic photo:** the very tall broad-topped photo is the firestorm (pyrocumulus) cloud, hours later — do not use it for the first 10 minutes.

### UI blurbs
- **RU:** 6 августа 1945 г. в 8:15 бомбардировщик B-29 «Энола Гэй» сбросил «Малыша» на Хиросиму; примерно через 44 с бомба взорвалась на высоте около 600 м. Мощность — около 15 кт (оценки 12–18 кт), через секунду огненный шар достиг почти 400 м в поперечнике, а облако за несколько минут поднялось примерно на 12 км. Пожары и «чёрный дождь» последовали в первые часы; к концу 1945 г. погибли около 140 000 человек.
- **EN:** At 8:15 on 6 August 1945 the B-29 Enola Gay dropped Little Boy on Hiroshima; about 44 seconds later it exploded roughly 600 m above the city. The yield was about 15 kt (estimates 12–18 kt); within a second the fireball grew to nearly 400 m across, and within minutes the cloud rose to about 12 km. Fires and "black rain" followed within hours, and about 140,000 people had died by the end of 1945.
