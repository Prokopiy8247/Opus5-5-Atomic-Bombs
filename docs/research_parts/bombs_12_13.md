# Research: bombs 12 (Crossroads "Baker") and 13 (Storax/Plowshare "Sedan")

Compiled 2026-11-07 by a research subagent. The data covers history and visual effects only: yield, burst geometry, outer dimensions, column, cloud, base surge, waves, crater, light and timing. It contains no weapon-design information.
Status keys: **MEASURED** means instrument or photogrammetric data from a primary or official compilation. **ESTIMATED** means derived by scaling or by eye, or published as an explicit estimate. **DISPUTED** means credible sources disagree; a recommendation follows each such row.
Unit notes: 1 ft = 0.3048 m and 1 yd = 0.9144 m. Baker heights are above sea level; the 1946 MSL heights equal heights above the lagoon. Sedan heights are given above ground (AGL) unless marked MSL; the ground at Sedan is at about 4,300 ft (1,310 m) MSL.

### Visual signature (Baker), stage by stage

The game camera default is 25 km away. Everything below is relative to surface zero (SZ) at sea level, with the sun 39° up in the ENE and light SE wind.

1. **0-0.02 s: hidden flash.** No fireball in the air. The lagoon surface over SZ glows blue-white from below, diffused as if through frosted glass ("ground-glass screen"), for 5-20 ms. Add a faint bloom pulse with no thermal or light flash on the landscape. Skip the white-out and exposure adaptation used for air bursts.
2. **0.004-0.1 s: slick and crack.** A **dark ring** (the slick, darker than the lagoon blue) expands at about 1,500 m/s. Just behind it a **white circular "crack"** patch spreads. The **spray dome** rises over SZ: a white, slightly glowing mound going up at 760 m/s that decelerates fast. For a few frames its top shows a bright point of fireball light (ref 02).
3. **0.01-1 s: column eruption.** The hollow **column** punches through the dome. It is white-grey, rough and spray-textured, already about 600 m wide, with its top at about 640 m by 0.75 s. Ragged jets form a "crown" on top.
4. **0.5-3.5 s: Wilson cloud.** A smooth, **milky-white hemisphere** (no texture, soft edges) inflates around the column with the air shock: about 1.6 km diameter and 0.8 km tall at about 1.2-1.5 s. It completely hides the column, and only the crown pokes out of its top (ref 04). Under it, a flat white disk marks the air-shock zone on the water. From about 2 s it thins **from the bottom up**: hemisphere → disk → doughnut. It is gone near the water by about 3-3.5 s and reveals the column and fleet. A flat, very wide remnant sheet hangs at cumulus-base height (about 500-800 m) and fades over about 10-15 s (ref 01).
5. **3-10 s: column and cauliflower.** The column stands as a vertical, slightly flared cylinder about 600 m wide. It is white-grey with darker grey-brown streaks of lagoon-bottom sediment and shadow. Its top disappears into a **cauliflower head**: lumpy, textured, white to light grey with a faint tan cast. The head is 1.5 km wide at 5 s, with its top at 1.14 km at 5 s and 1.4 km at 10 s. Spike-like jets shoot sideways out of the column walls and arc down (10-24 m/s fall).
6. **9-30 s: waves.** The first wave is a breaking ring of ±14 m at 305 m radius at 11 s. Ships nearby pitch violently, for example a carrier stern rising 13 m at 9 s. The ring expands at about 25 m/s (radius 610 m at 23 s, 1.2 km at 48 s) and shrinks in height per H ≈ 8,730/R m. A train of 3-20 concentric waves follows.
7. **10-35 s: collapse and base-surge birth.** At 10-12 s the column's outer shell falls back. Heavy **dark-grey "mammillary" lobes and rain curtains** hang from the column and head (ref 03 and the NOL crown-subsidence photo). The column narrows to about 450 m and becomes a translucent mist by about 33 s. Where the falling water hits the sea, a **brilliant white, billowing, ground-hugging fog wall** (the base surge) bursts outward. It starts at 320-370 m radius, 130 m tall, and moves at 37 m/s. Its front first slopes inward, then becomes rounded like a fog bank by 60 s.
8. **12-150 s: head collapse.** From 12 s the cauliflower sags and rains down in lobes. The first rain hits the sea at about 1 min. The rain falls in a ring 0.6-0.9 km from SZ, and by 2.5 min the head is mostly gone, with only remnants aloft. Top height: 1.74-1.86 km at 30 s, **2.32 km at 60 s**, about 3.0 km maximum while dispersing. Head diameter is 2.6 km at 60 s. There is never a stem or a classic mushroom.
9. **10-240 s: base surge growth.** A white-to-pale-grey doughnut-shaped fog. Radius R(t) = (3,600 + 3,700·t)/(90 + t) m: 0.92 km at 30 s, 1.44 km at 60 s, 2.14 km at 2 min, 2.49 km at 3 min, about 2.6 km at 4 min. Height rises from 130 m to 300 m at 60 s and 550 m from 140 s. It is egg-shaped downwind (add +U·t downwind, -U·t upwind, U ≈ 3 m/s). The centre clears after about 45-60 s (inner diameter about 2/3 of the outer). Ships vanish inside it.
10. **3.5-60 min: aftermath.** From about 210 s the surge lifts off the water (its base is 460 m up at 4.5 min). It turns into a **grey stratocumulus deck** with its top thickening towards about 1.8 km, drifting downwind at about 3 m/s. **Rain shafts** fall from it for about an hour. The lagoon is covered in concentric swell rings, and 2-5 m surf hits Bikini beach at about 5 min.

**Colours.** No technical source describes colours. The palette comes from the colour film (JTF-1 Kodachrome footage, used in *Radio Bikini* and *Trinity and Beyond*), from the greyscale photos, and from the physics. Lagoon: deep turquoise to navy. Slick: darker blue-grey. Crack, spray dome, Wilson cloud and base surge: pure to milky white. Column: white-grey with grey-brown sediment streaks. Cauliflower: white with a light grey to faint tan (calcareous mud) cast. Falling lobes and rain curtains: dark slate grey, backlit. Late surge deck: grey stratocumulus. There is **no orange, red or brown fire colour** at any stage, and no glow after about 0.05 s.

**Gameplay contrast with the other bombs.** This is the only water burst in the set. Make the Wilson dome, the hollow column and the creeping white base surge the "hero" effects. There is no thermal flash and no fireball.

### Disputes resolved (Baker)
- **Lagoon depth 55 vs 70 m:** use **55 m (180 ft)**. DASA-1251, the NPS archaeology report and Wikipedia agree: the device was 90 ft down and 90 ft above the floor. Glasstone's "about 200 ft" is rounding. No source for 70 m was found.
- **Yield 21 vs 23 kt:** use **23 kt** (DASA-1251, Wikipedia, NWA). "21 kt" is an early figure still used in some captions.
- **Wilson cloud timing:** Glasstone gives "1-2 s, gone a second later" for the Bikini tests in general; for Baker the column was hidden about 2 s; a NOL slide shows a hemisphere at about 0.8 s. Use: visible from 0.5 s, full at 1.2-1.5 s, cleared from the water by 3-3.5 s, with the upper remnant fading by about 15 s.
- **Cloud top 2.3 vs 3.0 km:** both are right at different times. Use **2.3 km at 60 s and 3.0 km maximum** while dispersing.
- **Cauliflower width 1.8 vs 2.6 km:** this is time-dependent. Use 1.5 km at 5 s, 1.8 km at about 15-20 s, and 2.6 km at 60 s.
- **First wave "94 ft":** this is the crest-to-trough height. The crest itself was about +14 m above still water.
- **Base surge "3.5 miles across" vs 5.1 km measured:** use the measured mean radius of **2.56 km** (5.1 km diameter), slightly egg-shaped downwind.
- **Water mass 1 vs 2 million t:** use "about a million tons" (Glasstone); Wikipedia's 2 Mt includes spray and sand.

### Visual signature (Sedan), stage by stage

The game camera default is 35 km away; this is better at 8-15 km. GZ is on a flat tan desert at 1.3 km MSL, the sun is 51° up in the east, and the sky is clear.

1. **0-0.24 s: nothing visible.** No flash, no fireball. Optionally, a camera seismometer "thump" arrives later with distance; this is a design choice.
2. **0.24-1.3 s: ground jump (spall).** The ground over GZ suddenly heaves. A wide disc, about 400-600 m (estimated), of surface dust and creosote bushes jumps into a thin **tan haze layer** a few metres thick, faster near the centre. The surface over GZ rises at about 32 m/s and then decelerates.
3. **1.3-3.0 s: dome.** A smooth, **pale-tan alluvium dome** swells. It re-accelerates to about 40 m/s and is about 90 m high and 180-250 m wide by 3 s. Keyframes for height: 0.5 s ≈ 8 m, 1.0 s ≈ 20 m, 1.5 s ≈ 33 m, 2.0 s ≈ 52 m, 2.5 s ≈ 72 m, 3.0 s ≈ 90 m (interpolated, ESTIMATED). The surface cracks radially, and dust streams from the cracks.
4. **3.0-4.5 s: venting.** The dome bursts. Through the cracks at its top, **incandescent gas glows salmon-orange to peach** for about 1-2 s (refs 02 and 03). This is the only "fire" in the event, a dull glow against daylight. The dome breaks into a spiky "bush" of dark clods and dust jets.
5. **4-15 s: ejecta fountain.** Rock and soil are thrown upward and outward. The central mass is a **dark grey-brown** lumpy column, with dozens of **cream-white ballistic streamers** (clods trailing fine dust) arcing out and down like a fountain or a palm crown (ref 01). The top of the ejecta reaches about **610 m** by about 12-15 s (ESTIMATED from the ballistics of a 610 m apex). Large blocks land out to about 1.7 km and kick up small dust puffs.
6. **10-40 s: fallback and base surge birth.** Most of the ejecta falls back into the forming crater and onto the lip. The falling curtain drags air and dust down, and where it hits the ground a **beige-grey dust surge** bursts outward in all directions, hugging the ground. It is initially about 100-300 m tall and moves at about 30-50 m/s (ESTIMATED by analogy with Baker's 37 m/s and with Glasstone's "fast-moving").
7. **40 s-6 min: base surge spread.** The surge expands as a low, billowing **tan-grey dust ring** about 200-500 m tall, decelerating. Suggested ESTIMATED schedule (Baker-like profile scaled to the measured final extent): radius about 1 km at 40-60 s, 2 km at about 1.5-2 min, 3 km at about 3 min, final **≈4 km crosswind / 3.2 km upwind** at about 4-6 min, then drifting north. Fig. 2.97 shows it wider than the main cloud at 6 min.
8. **15 s-6 min: main cloud.** The hot, wet gases and fine dust from the crater rise as a **grey-brown, lumpy, cauliflower-textured dust tower**. It has no cap and no ring; it gets lighter and browner as it thins. Suggested ESTIMATED schedule: 1.0 km AGL at 30 s, 2.0 km at 1.5 min, 3.0 km at 3 min, **3.7 km AGL** at about 5-6 min, where it flattens under the inversion. Width is about 1.5-2 km at 6 min (estimated). It drifts N to NNE at about 19 km/h, with a low brown dust haze over GZ for about 1 hour.
9. **After the dust clears.** A clean, conical **pale-tan funnel crater**, 370 m across at the original ground and 98 m deep, with walls at about the angle of repose. It has a hummocky lip 6-30 m high and **radial ejecta dunes and rays** extending about 0.5-1.7 km (ref 04). Splash craters from flying blocks are scattered around.

**Colours.** From the colour photo XX-13 (ref 01), with the film frames 02-03 corrected for their magenta fade. Sky: clear mid-blue. Desert: tan to light ochre with dark-olive creosote dots. Dome: pale tan. Ejecta column: dark grey-brown, slate in shadow. Streamers: cream-white. Base surge: beige-grey. Venting glow: salmon-orange to peach, short. Crater: pale tan or bone. **No fireball, no white-out, no condensation cloud.**

### Disputes resolved (Sedan)
- **Crater diameter 370 vs 390 m:** model a **370 m apparent rim** (NRHP 1,214 ft; Glasstone radius 611 ft; PNE-203F and PNE-213F about 1,200 ft) and put the lip crest at about 390-400 m. 1,280 ft (DOE fact sheet, Wikipedia) is most likely the lip-crest or a rounded figure. The UI can say "≈370-390 m".
- **Depth 98 vs 100 m:** use **98 m** (320-323 ft in every primary source).
- **Cloud height 3.7 km vs "16,000 ft":** **3.7 km above ground** (PNE-203F, DNA 6029F). 16,000 ft is MSL (ground is at 4,300 ft MSL), and the UPI 15,000 ft is a press estimate.
- **Venting at 3 s vs "shortly before 4 s":** use 3.0 s for dome break-up and 3.0-4.0 s for the visible venting glow.
- **Device mass 212 vs 158 kg:** use **212 kg** (Sublette/Wikipedia; size 43 × 96.5 cm agrees in both). It is cosmetic only, because the device is buried.
- **Time 09:00 vs 10:00:** both are the same instant, 09:00 PST = 10:00 PDT = 17:00 UTC. Use 10:00 local, because the AEC used PDT.

## Notes for the integrator
- Neither event has an air fireball. The renderer must be able to suppress the fireball, flash and thermal pulse completely for #12 and #13, keeping only the underwater glow (#12) or the venting glow (#13).
- The base surge is the signature effect of both. Baker: white water-mist, measured R(t) formula, 5.1 km final diameter. Sedan: tan dust, about 8 km final diameter (4 km radius), estimated timing. Glasstone 1977 §2.75 gives the general shape: a hollow cylinder with inner diameter about 2/3 of the outer, 300-600 m tall.
- Baker needs a water surface that can show a moving dark slick ring, a white crack disk, a solitary breaking wave ring and a concentric swell train.
- Sedan needs a terrain deformation, or an animated crater mesh swap after the dust hides the ground: dome → burst → crater with lip and rays.
- Neither bomb should be replaced. Both are among the best-documented events in the list, with photogrammetric time series for Baker and multiple primary DOE/DNA reports for Sedan.
