# Research: bombs 10 (Upshot-Knothole "Grable") and 11 ("Trinity")

Compiled 2026-11-07 by a research subagent. The data covers history and visual effects only: yield, burst geometry, outer dimensions, fireball, cloud, crater, light and sound. It contains no weapon-design information.
Status keys: **MEASURED** means instrument or photogrammetric data from a primary or official compilation. **ESTIMATED** means derived by scaling or by eye, or published as an estimate. **DISPUTED** means credible sources disagree; a recommendation follows each such row.
"AGL" means above ground level, the height the game should use. "MSL" means above mean sea level, which is how the 1950s and 1960s compilations quote cloud heights.

### Visual signature (Grable)
A low, dusty, "dirty" airburst over a pale dry lake on a bright morning, with the sun about 34° up in the east and cirrus in a blue sky. **0-1 ms:** a blue-white point flash 160 m up, saturating the camera. **1-14 ms:** a sphere grows (32 m @1 ms, 81 m @10 ms, about 92 m @14 ms). It turns from blinding white to a darker, opaque orange-red, mottled surface at the first thermal minimum (13-15 ms), when the image visibly dims. **15-55 ms:** brightness rises again. The lakebed under the ball "popcorns": thermally heated surface dust and smoke flash and fume, and black and white experimental smoke screens are visible near the ground. **about 55 ms:** the shock reaches the ground at GZ. **70-100 ms:** the reflected shock rises as a pale dome under the fireball (see `grable_reflected_shock.jpg`), hits the ball's underside, and flattens its bottom. **130 ms:** second maximum, a white-to-yellow-white ball of radius about 170 m whose flattened bottom nearly touches the ground. **0.13-3 s:** the Mach stem forms about 160 m out and the triple point rises slowly. Ahead of the main front, a ragged, turbulent dust wall 30-100 m high (the precursor) races outward over the lakebed to about 1.5 km. Its front is irregular and dust-laden rather than a crisp ring, and smoke-rocket trails kink and break as it passes. **1-5 s:** the ball goes yellow, then orange, and starts rising. Per the eyewitness it is less colourful and fades faster than usual. A wide grey-tan dust skirt churns up beneath it. **5-30 s:** an orange-brown cap (nitrogen-oxide tint) with a glowing orange toroidal ring rolls over. A thick dark-brown dust stem is drawn up by afterwinds, and the white/grey ground skirt spreads (see the two colour photos). About 30 s, the blast reaches the gun 10-11 km away. **1-9 min:** the cloud climbs to 4.1 km (1 min), 6.1 (2), 8.4 (4), 9.6 (6) and 9.7 km (7 min, 35,000 ft MSL), then levels at about 10 km. The cap turns from brown to white-grey. Very strong SW winds (about 90-140 mph aloft) shear the top and carry it NE quickly, and three successive white ice caps form on the column, leaving an "ice skirt" partway up the stem. A dust storm lingers over Frenchman Flat afterwards. Differences from Little Boy (#9): the burst height is 3.7× lower, there is a precursor and Mach stem, the stem and skirt are dusty and brown, and the cloud is sheared.

### Blurb
- **RU:** 25 мая 1953 года 280-мм пушка M65 «Atomic Annie» выстрелила ядерным снарядом мощностью 15 кт над пустыней Френчмен-Флэт в Неваде. Через 19 секунд снаряд взорвался в 160 м над землёй, более чем в 10 км от орудия. Это единственный ядерный снаряд в истории, действительно выпущенный из пушки. Низкий взрыв над раскалённой пустыней породил «предвестник» — пыльную ударную волну, которая обогнала основную и разбила технику сильнее, чем ожидали.
- **EN:** On 25 May 1953 the 280 mm M65 "Atomic Annie" cannon fired a 15-kiloton nuclear shell over Frenchman Flat, Nevada. The shell burst 19 seconds later, 160 m above the ground and more than 10 km from the gun. It is the only nuclear shell ever actually fired from a gun. The low burst over hot desert produced a "precursor", a dust-laden shock front that outran the main blast wave and wrecked vehicles worse than expected.

### Resolutions of disputed points

1. **Trinity yield: 21 kt vs 24.8 kt (and lower outliers).** The low values (about 10 kt from blast gauges and Fermi; 9.1 kt in the 2026 preprint) are known to under-read or count only part of the fissions: blast methods read low at Trinity, and the preprint explicitly matches only the core fissions. All radiochemical and optical estimates fall between 16.8 and 24.8 kt. The 2021 LANL value (24.8 ± 2 kt) overlaps the DOE value (21 ± 2 kt) within the stated uncertainties.
   - **Game value: 21 kt** (the official DOE number). It keeps the strict yield order: Grable 15 < Trinity 21 < Crossroads Baker 23 kt. Using 24.8 kt would put Trinity above Baker.
   - The visible difference is negligible: the fireball radius changes by (24.8/21)^0.4 = +7 % and the cloud height by a few percent.
   - UI text: "21 kt (official); a 2021 LANL re-analysis gives 24.8 ± 2 kt".

2. **Trinity cloud top: 11.6 km (DOE) vs 14-17 km+ (LANL / Warren).** Every instrumented or official contemporaneous figure falls in 35,000-41,000 ft MSL:
   - DASA 1251 curve: 35,000 ft, still rising at 10 min.
   - Groves memo: 41,000 ft, 36,000 ft above ground.
   - LANL/DOE: 38,000 ft.
   - McMillan: about 8 mi.

   With the site at 4,624 ft, this is **9.3-11.0 km above ground**. The 50,000-70,000 ft (15-21 km) figures come from Warren's report, which explicitly says "variously estimated", and from press reports. They also exceed what a 20-25 kt cloud reaches in summer: 02_PHYSICS gives 10-12 km for 15-25 kt.
   - **Game: final top 10.5 km AGL (≈ 39,000 ft MSL), reached at about 11-12 min** after the DASA rise curve. Cloud base 1.8 km AGL. Cap Ø about 4.5 km.
   - 01_BOMBS_REFERENCE should replace "LANL 45-55 kft (14-17 km)" with "Warren 50-70 kft, visual estimate, rejected".

3. **Trinity crater (80 m × 1.4 m vs 5 ft × 30 ft vs 1,100 ft × 9.5 ft vs half a mile).** These are different nested features, not contradictions:
   - a central scoured bowl 40-90 m across and 1.4-1.8 m deep;
   - a shallow saucer about 335-366 m across, up to about 2.9 m deep, where vegetation and topsoil were removed;
   - a dark fused-glass zone about 600 m across (the "half-mile"), visible on the 28 h aerial photo.
   - **Game: 70 m × 1.8 m bowl inside a 350 m × 2.5 m saucer, inside a 600 m dark/green trinitite disc.**

4. **Trinity fireball radius vs time.** The 02_PHYSICS values "138 m @25 ms, 200 m @53 ms, 218 m @90 ms" do not match the measured Taylor/Mack data: 130 m @25 ms, 175 m @53 ms, and 216 m @90 ms from Mack's fit.
   - The 90 ms value happens to agree, but 25 ms and 53 ms are 6 % and 14 % too large.
   - The "79 m @6 ms" Sedov check is fine; measured is about 75 m.
   - **Use the Taylor table above.** After the 16 ms breakaway, the luminous ball lags the shock and grows slowly to about 300 m radius by 1-2 s.

5. **Trinity colours.** Resolved above: violet-blue-white → white → yellow → orange-red → dull red → purple glow. "Green" is mostly a welding-glass artifact (Morrison) or a brief, minor tint (Rabi). The recommended "purple" is real: radioluminescent glow seen by several witnesses and by Mack, at the start (violet) and at about 30-35 s.

6. **Trinity "skirt", "popcorn", "rope trick" (marked unconfirmed in 01_BOMBS_REFERENCE).** All three are **confirmed** by Mack's optical summary in LA-6300-H:
   - spikes below the equator (rope-trick-like);
   - "a wide skirt of lumpy matter" rising from the ground ahead of the Mach wave (visible as the lumpy band in the 16 ms photo);
   - a smooth Mach belt.

7. **Trinity detonation time.** Use 05:29:45 MWT (LANL, DOE, Army, NWA). The 05:29:21 ± 15 s value is a minority figure; the difference does not matter for the dawn lighting.

8. **Trinity sound at 9 km "about 40 s" (01_BOMBS_REFERENCE).** This is Farrell's estimate. Physics (and Fermi and Morrison at 16 km: 40-45 s) puts arrival at 9.1 km at about **27 s**. The game should use the physics module.

9. **Grable precursor and Mach stem.**
   - Precursor: documented by Wikipedia, by DNA 6014F (Project 8.12b precursor experiment at Encore/Grable, plus smoke-screen tests), by Glasstone's precursor criteria (Grable's scaled HOB of 212 ft is far below the 800 ft limit), and by secondary accounts of unexpected vehicle damage.
   - Mach stem: Glasstone's general rule puts its start at a ground distance about equal to the HOB, about 160 m. No Grable-specific triple-point measurement was found, so the triple-point height curve is an estimate.

10. **Fireball timing formulas (02_PHYSICS).** Measured first thermal minima are:
    - Grable 15 kt: 13.3-14.9 ms;
    - Upshot-Knothole Badger 23 kt (same DASA source): 16.8-19.2 ms;
    - Trinity about 21 kt: about 16 ms.

    The doc formula t_min ≈ 0.0022·W^0.5 s (8.5 ms at 15 kt) is about 40 % early.
    - **Recommend t_min ≈ 0.0036·W^0.5 s** (13.9 / 16.5 / 17.3 ms for 15 / 21 / 23 kt).
    - The t₂ formula 0.039·W^0.45 matches Grable (132 vs 122-138 ms) and Badger (160 vs 155 ms), so keep it.

### Visual signature (Trinity)
**Pre-dawn**, 05:29:45: the sun is 7.5° below the horizon in the ENE, the sky is deep blue-violet with a paler band low in the east, broken grey cloud lies overhead after the night's rain, and the desert is almost dark. A 30 m tower with a small tin shed stands at GZ. **0-0.65 ms:** a violet/blue-white point flash brighter than anything else, and the scene's own light jumps from twilight to "brighter than midday" (exposure must clamp hard). The ball grows from 11 m @0.1 ms to 31 m @0.65 ms, when it touches the ground. A few short **spikes** jab out below its equator, the surface shows **blisters**, and the lower part is brighter. **1-11 ms:** the bubble becomes a smooth, mottled **hemisphere** (37 m @1 ms, 49 m @2 ms, 67 m @4.6 ms). A bright line sweeps up it like a rising curtain from 3 to 11 ms. From about 2 ms a **bright lumpy skirt** ("popcorn") boils up from the ground ahead of the front, with a smooth bright **Mach belt** above it. **11-30 ms:** the dome reaches 106 m @15 ms and 130 m @25 ms (about 220 m wide, 140 m tall at 16 ms). It dims to an opaque orange-red at the **thermal minimum (about 16 ms)**, when the luminous ball drops behind the now-invisible shock. **30 ms-1 s:** a dark brownish (nitrogen-oxide) front rides just behind the shock to about 375 m. The shock is visible only as refraction, 240 m @0.1 s and 460 m @0.39 s. Brightness climbs to the **second maximum (about 0.15 s)**: blinding white, then yellow-white, as the hemisphere grows to about **300 m radius (600 m wide) by 1-2 s**. The lower part is progressively hidden by the dust cloud growing out of the skirt (Morrison: dark matter eating into the disc from below at 2-3 s). The overhead clouds are lit red-orange from below (Aeby photo), and a **hole opens in the cloud deck**. **2-15 s:** the top starts rising at 2 s, colours shift yellow → orange, and the surface boils into a cauliflower shape. At **3.5 s** a neck forms one-third of the way up and the upper part rolls into a **vortex ring**. The stem twists like a left-handed screw, and **two condensation (fog) rings** appear above the column. **15-35 s:** the glow fades to orange-red, then a dull red ball whose top rises at 57 m/s, reaching 1.5 km at about 26 s. At about 30 s the whole surface shows a **violet-purple radioluminescent glow for about 5 s**, and a faint purple haze surrounds the cloud. Shock and sound arrive at 27 s (9.1 km bunker), 44 s (base camp, 16 km) and 92 s (Compania Hill). **1-12 min:** a red-brown cloud, whitening as it cools, climbs to 2.1 km (1 min), 3.3 (2), 5.3 (4), 6.9 (6), 8.2 (8), 9.5 (10) and about 10.5 km (11-12 min). It pauses and spreads at the inversion (about 3.8 km AGL) before punching through, which leaves a three-layer structure: a low dust sheet spreading many km and drifting NE, a mid layer at the inversion, and a white-grey cap about 4.5 km wide at the top drifting slowly NW. The base stays dark red-brown. **About 20 min:** the sun reaches the cloud top (pink-gold alpenglow) while the ground is still in twilight; sunrise is at 06:07. **Ground afterwards:** the tower is gone. There is a 70 m bowl inside a 350 m shallow saucer inside a 600 m dark disc of **light-green glassy trinitite**, with a pale "frosted" ground beyond. Jumbo stands intact 730 m NW with its tower collapsed.

- **EN:** On 16 July 1945 at 05:29 local time, before dawn, the first nuclear device in history, the "Gadget", was set off on a 30 m steel tower in the New Mexico desert. Its official yield is 21 kt; a 2021 Los Alamos re-analysis gives 24.8 ± 2 kt. For a few seconds the flash lit the mountains brighter than day, and the cloud rose more than 10 km. The heat fused the desert sand into green glass, later named trinitite.
