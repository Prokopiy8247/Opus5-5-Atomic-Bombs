# Research part: bombs 04-06 (Grand Slam, GBU-43/B MOAB, W54 Davy Crockett / Little Feller I) + BLU-82 backup

Researched 2026-11-07. Scope: public historical and visual data only (yield, burst height, fireball/cloud size and timing, crater, appearance, outer dimensions, mass, paint). No design details.

Status keys: **MEASURED** = instrument or official measurement/record; **ESTIMATED** = derived, scaled, or estimated from imagery; **DISPUTED** = sources disagree (a recommendation is given).

## Summary of corrections to `docs/01_BOMBS_REFERENCE.md` (for the merge agent)

1. **Little Feller I burst height: RESOLVED to 3 ft (0.9 m).** Three independent official documents agree: DOE/NV-209 Rev 16 (HOB column "3"), DOE/NV-1200 ("detonated three feet above the surface"), and DNA 6027F (shot synopsis "Height of burst: three feet above ground"). DNA 6027F also explains where the 20-40 ft figure comes from. The original plan had a tactical shot set for a 40 ft burst, but before firing it "was changed to a three-foot shot". The W54 Mod 2 fuze had a 2 ft or 40 ft height-of-burst switch. Wikipedia's 20-40 ft and Sublette's "Surface, 40 feet" are the pre-shot plan, not the event. **Game: 0.9 m (round to 1 m).**
2. **Little Feller I yield.** DOE/NV-209 Rev 16 gives only "Low", so the "18 t from DOE tables" claim in 01_BOMBS_REFERENCE is not quite right. 18 t comes from Sublette (Nuclear Weapon Archive) and Wikipedia. 20 t is the programmed/nominal W54 Mod 2 yield (Hansen; *Safety Rules for Peacetime Operations*, 1961). **Game: 18 t (0.018 kt).** UI may say "≈18-20 t".
3. **Little Feller I cloud top 11,000 ft is above sea level, not above ground.** DNA 6027F states that altitudes are measured from sea level. Ground zero is at 5,178 ft MSL (DOE/NV-209), so the cloud rose about 5,800 ft, **≈1.8 km above the ground**. The companion shot Little Feller II (22 t) also reached 11,000 ft MSL from 5,136 ft. Note for the physics module: the sub-kt formula in 02_PHYSICS (H ≈ 2.7·W^0.33 km gives 0.72 km) **under-predicts these two measured shots by about 2.5×** (hot desert day, 29.7-35.5 °C). Use the measured 1.8 km.
4. **Little Feller I firing distance: 2,853 m, not 852 m.** DNA 6027F gives 2,853 m and Brookings gives 9,357 ft (2,852 m). Wikipedia gives 1.7 mi (2.7 km). DOE/NV-1200's "2,796 feet" (= 852 m) is almost certainly a feet/metres slip: the M29 heavy launcher range is 4 km, and the crew fired from an M113 well behind the observers' trench line. Observers' bleachers were ≈3.5 km SW of GZ (DNA 6027F; DOE/NV-1200 gives 2.17 mi).
5. **MOAB smoke colour.** The USAF test video (21 Nov 2003) shows a yellow-white/orange flattened fireball that within ≈2-3 s becomes a **white to light-grey** cloud (aluminised H-6 gives white Al2O3 smoke plus dust), with darker grey only in the lower lobes. It is **not** the "lots of black smoke" TNT palette. Use bright orange flame, then white/grey smoke.
6. **MOAB cloud "10,000 ft" is a press figure.** A local newspaper used the word "reported", and pre-test articles said it was expected. A Pentagon official who reviewed the tape said it was a tall debris cloud that did *not* look like a nuclear mushroom. **Game: ≈1.5 km top (ESTIMATED; see reasoning).** "Visible from up to 20 miles (32 km)" is from an official Eglin photo caption.
7. **MOAB first live test (11 March 2003) used tritonal**, per Wikipedia citing GlobalSecurity. The production weapon and the 21 Nov 2003 live test used H-6. The 11 March test article was painted bright orange with black stripes (DoD photo).
8. **Grand Slam crater is DISPUTED:** 124 ft × 34 ft (38 × 10 m; witness/Wikipedia/Bomber Command Museum) vs 130 ft × 70 ft (40 × 21 m; Geograph, Dambusters blog, Memorial Flight Club) vs 140 × 70 ft (NF Knowledge). **Game: 38 m × 10 m.** This is consistent with cube-root cratering scaling for ≈6.6 t TNT at near-optimum depth in soil. The 70 ft depth is unlikely for an *apparent* crater. Also there is **no source for an "orange flash"** from Grand Slam. The buried bursts showed earth, dust and gas venting.

### Visual signature (game)
The Grand Slam has no fireball, so it is the "earthquake" bomb of the lineup. Play the delivery first: a 7.8 m dark-green bomb falls almost vertically, spinning slowly, and at about 320 m/s it hits soft ground with a dull thud and a small puff of dust. Optional short fuze delay of 1-2 s for drama; the real delay at Bielefeld was ≈11 s.
- **t = 0:** detonation at −20 m. There is no light. The ground shock comes first: a sharp seismic jolt, camera shake, and a ripple ring in the soil spreading out at ≈1-1.5 km/s.
- **0-0.3 s:** the ground surface heaves into a dome ≈35-40 m wide and ≈5-8 m high, with cracks radiating out. The air blast is weak and muffled.
- **0.3-1.0 s:** the dome ruptures. Jets of dark brown earth, stones and grey-white gas burst upward and outward in radial "fingers", which form the dark streaks seen in the Arnsberg photo. There may be a brief dull orange glint deep in the vent (≤0.2 s), but keep it tiny.
- **1-4 s:** the ejecta column rises to ≈150-250 m, brown-grey at the base and lighter grey-white at the billowing cauliflower top (≈130 m across). Heavy clods arc out to ≈150-200 m and fall back as a rain of soil, raising a dust skirt.
- **4-30 s:** the column collapses. A light-brown/grey dust cloud ≈300-400 m high drifts downwind, and the ground is buried in fallback.
- **30 s and later:** the crater (Ø 38 m, 10 m deep, raised lip) is visible through thinning dust. Sporadic gas puffs and small secondary "pops" vent from the crater for minutes ("like a volcano", per the eyewitness).
- **Sound:** a deep, low thump with ground rumble, not a sharp crack. At 5 km the shock arrives ≈14.6 s after detonation. Colours are earth tones (#6b5a45 soil, #9a8f80 dust, #d8d4cc grey-white gas). There is no thermal flash.

**Reasoning for MOAB cloud top (1.5 km).** The two measured near-surface low-yield nuclear shots of 1962 (Little Feller I, 18 t, and II, 22 t) both reached 11,000 ft MSL, about **1.8 km above ground**. MOAB's total heat release is of the same order: ≈8.5 t H-6 with aluminium afterburn ≈ 5-6·10¹⁰ J, against 7.5·10¹⁰ J for an 18 t nuclear shot of which ≈35 % radiates away. Buoyant rise scales roughly with Q^1/4. That gives ≈1.3-1.7 km on a warm Florida afternoon. 3 km would need about 10× more heat, so it is a press estimate. Cap diameter ≈0.5-0.6 km at ≈2-3 min.

### Visual signature (game)
The MOAB's look is a **broad, low, golden pancake of flame that turns white**.
- **Delivery (optional):** an MC-130 at altitude extracts the 9.2 m olive bomb on a pallet. The drogue pulls the cradle away and the bomb drops nose-down with grid fins deployed.
- **t = 0** (burst ≈2 m above the ground): one frame of blue-white flash with large bloom, much brighter than the sky for ≤50 ms.
- **0.03-0.15 s:** a brilliant yellow-white hemispherical dome appears and spreads sideways much faster than it rises.
- **0.15-0.5 s:** it flattens to ≈140 m wide × 40 m high (aspect ≈3.5:1). The surface is turbulent and lumpy, with a white-yellow core and orange edges. The first dust kicks up at the rim and the shock ring becomes visible as a ground-level dust ring and, in humid air, a brief condensation flash.
- **0.5-1.5 s:** luminosity falls. The colour goes from deep orange to orange-red, and brown/dark-grey smoke blotches appear on the surface. The dust skirt races outward along the ground (≈250 m wide by 3 s).
- **1.5-3 s:** the flames die (last orange glints inside the cloud at ≈2-2.5 s). The cloud turns **white to light grey** (aluminium oxide smoke and dust) with darker grey lower lobes.
- **3-10 s:** a billowing cauliflower cloud rises as a thermal. By 8 s it is ≈120 m tall and ≈170 m wide, with no stem.
- **10 s to 3 min:** it keeps rising and spreading into a lumpy debris "mushroom" without a true stem, with a top ≈1.5 km and a cap ≈0.5 km, and slowly drifts. There is no crater, only a scorched, scoured disc ≈150-250 m wide with flattened vegetation.
- **Sound:** a sharp loud crack and a long rolling rumble. At 8 km it arrives after ≈23 s.

### Visual signature (game)
This is the smallest nuclear event of the lineup and the first "true nuclear" look. Keep it tiny but intensely bright, with a thin, disproportionately tall cloud.
- **Setting:** morning (10:00) high desert at 1,578 m altitude. Sagebrush flats, a dirt road crossing near GZ, low ridges on the horizon, hot hazy air. Wind blows toward the NNW at ≈7 m/s.
- **Launch (optional):** a muzzle blast with a white smoke trail from an M113 2.85 km away. The finned "watermelon" arcs for ≈10-15 s.
- **t = 0** (burst at 0.9 m): an instant blue-white point that blows out the exposure. The whole frame is white for ≈0.1-0.17 s at close range (this is what the film shows). Use the double pulse: a dip at ≈0.3 ms (not visible) and the second peak at ≈6 ms.
- **0.006-0.05 s:** a hemispherical fireball grows to R ≈17 m, white-yellow at ≈8,000 K-like brightness.
- **0.05-0.6 s:** it cools through yellow to orange and orange-red. A bright ring of dust erupts at the base and races out along the ground, and the road dust lights up as a bright horizontal line. The shock front is invisible except as this dust and a quick heat shimmer.
- **0.6-1.5 s:** the glow fades and the fireball becomes a white/grey, bell-shaped, "boiling" cloud ≈40-60 m tall sitting on the ground. Faint red-brown tint (little NO2 at this yield), with brownish desert dust at the bottom.
- **1.5-20 s:** the cloud lifts off as a rising column with a balled head. A thin dusty stem is pulled up by afterwinds, and the low dust skirt spreads to ≈300-500 m wide. By 20 s it is a small cauliflower cap ≈0.7 km up on a thin stem.
- **20 s-3 min:** the cap leans and drifts NNW, growing to ≈0.7 km across. The top stabilises at ≈1.8 km above the desert, white on top and dusty tan below. The cloud is out of proportion to the tiny fireball, which is the signature look of this shot.
- **Ground:** a small shallow crater ≈8 m, a scorched dark disc ≈30-40 m, and blown-down sagebrush to ≈100-200 m.
- **Sound:** a sharp "crack-boom" with a short rumble. From the observers at 3.5 km it arrives after ≈10 s; at the recommended 8 km, after ≈23 s.

- **EN:** "On 17 July 1962, US Army soldiers at the Nevada Test Site fired a live M388 projectile with a W54 warhead from a Davy Crockett recoilless gun mounted on an armoured personnel carrier. The 34 kg 'atomic watermelon' flew 2.85 km and exploded less than a metre above the ground with a yield of about 18 tons of TNT; its cloud rose about 1.8 km above the desert. Little Feller I was the last atmospheric nuclear explosion at the Nevada Test Site, watched by Robert F. Kennedy and General Maxwell Taylor."
