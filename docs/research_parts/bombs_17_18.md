### Visual signature (Ivy Mike)

**Setting.** Sunrise over Enewetak. The sun is 2° above the horizon in the ESE (az. 105°), so there is warm, low side light. Scattered trade cumulus ("scud") have bases ~0.6 km and tops ~1.5-2 km. Elugelab is a tiny, low, flat islet (~3 m above sea level) carrying a 27 × 14 × 19 m corrugated-aluminium shed, a 91 m lattice tower and a 2.7 km straight conduit/causeway to Boken. The lagoon is turquoise and the ocean side deep blue.

**t = 0-0.1 s.** Total blue-white flash (bloom/white-out). Within ~10 ms, **five jagged lightning channels** shoot up from the sea surface about 1 km from GZ into the clouds and writhe/kink within a millisecond; they are gone by ~0.08 s.

**0.1-1 s.** A hemispherical fireball sits flat on the sea, white with a mottled, patchy surface (EG&G high-speed frame). It reaches ~4.5 km across by 1 s.

**1-5 s.** It grows to a **maximum of ~5.2 km diameter** ("3¼ miles"). Seen from 30+ miles, it looks "like the sun when half-risen": a dome as wide as the horizon allows. Its colour shifts from white to yellow-white. At ~1-4 s a **huge Wilson condensation cloud** (cloud-chamber effect; ~6-10 km wide, my estimate) flashes white around and over it, and the low cumulus partly hide it.

**5-15 s.** A "momentary hover": the fireball hangs, then begins to lift. Its surface turns yellow-orange, then orange-red, with dark veins.

**10-90 s.** As the base lifts off the sea, a **ring-shaped curtain of water and spray** rises around the base of the stem and falls back where the island was. A wide, dirty stem forms: grey-brown coral dust and white spray, ~3-4 km wide. The rising mass becomes a toroidal, cauliflower cap, brilliant white on the sunlit side, and lights the cumulus deck below orange. **Top 13 km at 60 s** (cap ~9 km wide), **17 km at 90 s**: it pierces the tropopause (~16-17 km).

**~2.5 min.** At ships ~50 km away a sharp crack arrives, followed by a long, broken rumble.

**2.5-6 min.** The rise slows: 30 km at 2.5 min, 33 km at 3.5 min, ~36 km at 5.5 min. The cap "splashes" and flattens against the stratosphere. Its colour is white, with **reddish-brown tints in the shadowed folds** (nitrogen oxides). Smooth lenticular/pileus sheets cut across the stem.

**6-10 min.** Main top stabilises at **≈37 km** (120 000 ft). Optional effect: a narrow, sharp-pointed overshooting turret to ~41 km for a few minutes. The cap underside is ~20 km. **Cap diameter ~61 km at 10 min.**

**10-30 min.** The cap spreads to **~100 km by 30 min**. The lower cloud/stem broadens to ~30 km, and its junction with the cap (~14 km) shows "numerous projecting fingers". Winds start to shear it. On the ground, Elugelab is replaced by a **1.9 km × 50 m water-filled crater**, and the neighbouring islets are swept bare.

### Visual signature (Castle Bravo)

**Setting.** 06:45: deep-blue pre-dawn twilight. The sun is 8° below the eastern horizon, so there is only a faint glow in the east. The lagoon is dark and the reef surf pale. A broken deck of trade cumulus sits at 0.6-1.2 km, with a thin high cirrostratus veil and a few altostratus near 5.5 km. On the reef: the tiny Site 20 islet with a concrete blockhouse, a 1 km causeway to Namu, and small mirror towers in the shallows.

**t = 0-1 s.** A blinding flash turns the whole sky white; the light is seen 400 km away. A hemisphere grows on the reef to **~4.8 km across at 1 s**.

**1-10 s.** It keeps growing to **~6 km at 3.5 s** and a **maximum ~7 km** at ~8-10 s, white then yellow-white. "For a moment it seemed to cling to the earth." The cumulus deck, sea and sky glow **crimson-orange**: "flaming sunset colors" to observers 150 km away. Low pileus caps form over the swelling dome.

**10-60 s.** The fireball "springs into the sky", still glowing yellow at the core and orange outside, with a boiling cauliflower surface. **Stacked condensation rings appear** (the signature look): flat thin white/orange-lit discs at ~3-4 km and ~5 km altitude (0.35-0.8 min), ice-cap sheets draped over the rising bubble with undersides at ~6 and ~7 km and widths ~9-14 km (0.4-0.9 min), and ring 3 at ~8-9.5 km. Thin smoke-rocket trails streak up beside the stem. At **60 s: top 14.3 km, cap 11.6 km wide, underside ~7 km**, stem ~4 km wide (dirty grey-brown coral debris).

**1-2.5 min.** The third ice cap widens from 14 to 20 km at 6-7.6 km altitude. Ring 4 is dragged upward from 9 to 16 km. Two skirts (cone-shaped sheets at 5.5-8 km, widening 7→15 km) and three bells form around the stem. At 2 min the top is 25 km and the cap ~24 km wide. The fireball glow fades to dull red inside the cap at ~1.5-2 min, and the sky returns to dark twilight blue.

**2.5-5 min.** The top climbs to 31 km (3 min), 34 km (4 min) and **35 km max (~5 min)**. The stem flares into a **trumpet** shape and the head flattens into an anvil on the tropopause, with its underside at ~16 km by 4 min. The cap is ~44 km wide at 4 min and ~55 km at 5 min.

**5-10 min.** A central **thunderhead dome** pushes to **~39-40 km** (6-8 min). The anvil spreads at ~12 km per minute: ~67 km at 6 min, **100 km at ~8.7 min, 113 km at 10 min**, with the underside at ~19 km. The stem is ~8 km thick. Curtains of falling debris hang like **virga** under the anvil.

**~9-30 min (estimated, from solar geometry).** Because the cloud top at 35-40 km sits above the Earth's shadow, it catches **direct sunlight ~9 min after the shot** while the sea is still in twilight: give the anvil top a pink-gold rim light from the east. At 16 min the anvil is spreading. At 30 min the sun rises just left of the stem (07:15). The cap is ~160 km across by then (estimated) and drifts east.

**Ground.** A **2.0 km × 76 m crater** is blasted into the reef where Site 20 stood. Hours later, white coral "snow" (fallout) falls downwind.

## Disputes resolved (summary)

1. **Mike ceiling 36.6 vs 41 km.** 36.6 km (120 000 ft) is the best-supported top of the main cloud: contemporary aircraft sextant readings that stayed constant for an hour (S3), the official history (S2) and Sublette (S1). The 41 km (135 500 ft) figure is a single sighting of a sharp plume above the top (S3). A 1954 photogrammetric comparison (S4) gives only 30 km (98 000 ft), so the true main top was probably 30-37 km. **Game: main cap 37 km; optional thin turret to 41 km.**
2. **Mike cap width 100-160 km.** These figures have no measured time attached. Measured values: 9 km at 1 min and 61 km at 10 min (S4), and ~97 km ("sixty miles") at 30 min (S2, S1). "100 miles" (161 km) is the film's rounded figure (S5) and Wikipedia's "eventually". **Game: 61 km @10 min, 100 km @30 min, up to 160 km only after 1 h.**
3. **Mike fireball 5.2 km.** Official film: "at its maximum … about 3¼ miles" (S5); NNSA caption: 3½ mi. Wikipedia's 2.9-3.3 km radius is a calculator value (S19). **Game: 5.2 km maximum diameter at ~3-5 s; ~4.5 km at 1 s.**
4. **Mike cloud-rise timing.** 12-14 km @1 min (S4, S3), 17 km @1.5 min (S2), ~30-33 km @2.6 min (S2, S3; preliminary), ~36 km @5.7 min (S2). Wikipedia's "one minute later 33 km" means 33 km at ~2.6 min.
5. **Bravo fireball 7.2 km at 1 s.** The timing is too fast: the documented wording is "within seconds … more than 3 mi". **Game: ~4.8 km @1 s → ~7 km max @8-10 s.**
6. **Bravo cloud timeline.** Confirmed with photogrammetry (S4): 14.3 km @1 min, main top 34.7 km @~5 min, thunderhead 39 km @6-8 min, 113 km wide @10 min (100 km @~8.7 min), stem ~4 → 8 km. "40 km at 6 min" refers to the thunderhead, not the flat top.
7. **Bravo rings, caps, skirts and bells.** Confirmed: 4 rings, 3 ice caps, 2 skirts and 3 bells (S4 §6.1), with measured heights and diameters (table above).
8. **Bravo crater 2 km × 76 m.** Confirmed by S11, S12, S13 (and "mile-wide", S10). Modern sonar shows it partly infilled.
9. **Crimson sky.** Qualitatively confirmed by the colour photos and by several witnesses (red, orange, "flaming sunset colors"). Keep it for the first ~2 min, then return to pre-dawn blue.
10. **Bravo yield ±5 Mt.** Not supported: the fireball-yield measurement was 14.8 ± 0.2 Mt (S4). Use 15 Mt; predicted 6 Mt.
