# 15 B83: reference images

All files were checked after download (real JPEG, opened and viewed). Only public-domain or CC0 material is kept.
No full-yield B83 explosion has ever taken place in the atmosphere, so the explosion reference below is an analog (a different weapon of similar yield).

| File | What it shows | Source page | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| `b83_trainer_pease_1989.jpg` (1280x853) | Full side view of a B83 trainer (inert, marked "TRAINING ONLY") on a yellow stand beside an FB-111, Pease AFB, 4 Oct 1989. Shows the slim gloss-white cylindrical body, the ogive nose ending in stepped concentric rings (the shock-absorbing frangible-ring nose), two suspension lugs on top, small black/red stencils, and four small swept tail fins on a short tail cone. USAF photo DF-ST-90-09842. | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:B83_nuclear_bomb_trainer.jpg | Public domain (USAF, MSgt Ken Hammond) |
| `b83_hill_museum_cc0.jpg` (1280x853) | B83 on an olive-drab handling cradle under an F-5E at Hill Aerospace Museum, Utah. A close view of the white finish, the nose rings, the rivet lines of the tail section, and the fins. | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:F5E_Tiger_II_B83_HAFB.jpg | CC0 1.0 (photographer Wilson44691) |
| `b83_release_f4c_1983.jpg` (1280x830) | An F-4C Phantom (64-0727) releasing a B83 flight-test unit over Edwards AFB, 23 Aug 1983 (last flight of the B83 test project). The test body is painted high-visibility red-orange. At the tail, the white parachute pack and pilot chutes are deploying, with a smoke trail. This is a test-unit colour scheme, not a service one. | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:B83_nuclear_bomb_test_with_F-4C_Phantom_1983.JPEG | Public domain (USAF / DefenseImagery DF-SC-84-08047) |
| `analog_castle_nectar_surface_burst_frames.jpg` (2040x228) | ANALOG: 5 frames from the US film of Castle Nectar (14 May 1954, 1.69 Mt surface burst on a barge, filmed from an aircraft above). Shows a near-surface megaton fireball with a dark debris-laden top, surrounded by a huge bright condensation (Wilson) dome and shock ring, with smoke-rocket trails. Nectar is the closest yield (1.69 Mt vs 1.2 Mt) among filmed US surface bursts. | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Operation_Castle_-_Nectar_-_Detonation.ogv | Public domain (US Government film). The frames are a derived still made with ffmpeg. |
